02 — Identity & Source of Truth

What if three systems call the same company three different things?

Connecting data requires more than importing it. The platform needs to identify when different records refer to the same real business object.

Source Records

Accounting:
Vendor #812 — ABC Materials LLC
Spreadsheet:
Row 146 — ABC Materials
PDF Invoice:
ABC MAT LLC
→

Canonical Supplier

Supplier ID: SUP-00418

Name: ABC Materials LLC

Status: Active

Linked source records remain attached so the system knows exactly where each piece of information originated.

Why this matters

The common data layer should preserve provenance. A standardized supplier record should still know which accounting vendor, spreadsheet row, invoice and external-system IDs support it.

(The system creates a common identity without erasing the original evidence.)

Example: Accounts Payable

A supplier invoice arrives by email. The document processor extracts the supplier, invoice number, date, PO number and amount. The platform identifies the supplier and locates the related purchase order and receiving record.

If the PO, receipt and invoice agree, the transaction can continue through the company's normal approval process. If they disagree, the exception is shown to accounting with the supporting records.

Human control

Accounting personnel remain responsible for review, corrections, exceptions, approvals and company-specific accounting judgment. Automation reduces searching, re-entry and repetitive comparisons.

04 — Automation Engine

Events tell us what happened. Rules decide what happens next.

Automation is built around company-defined triggers, conditions, actions and approval requirements.

WHEN Inventory falls below 100 units

Triggered by INVENTORY_CHANGED.

→
IF Item is active and no open PO already covers demand

The system evaluates business conditions.

→
THEN Create a purchase recommendation for review

A person can approve creation of the PO.

The company defines the boundary.

Some workflows may be safe to automate completely. Others may stop at a recommendation, task or approval screen. Permissions and approval rules determine who can authorize sensitive actions.

05 — Open Historical Data

The operational application and historical data layer serve different jobs.

Day-to-day transactions require a reliable operational database. Historical events and analytical data can also flow into an open, portable data layer.

Operational Layer

Current customers, suppliers, inventory, orders, invoices, payments and workflow state.

(Designed for live business transactions.)

→

Events / Data Pipeline

Changes are captured, transformed and written into historical analytical structures.

(Preserves what happened over time.)

→

Open Data Layer

Historical data can use open table formats and object storage for analytics, BI, external tools, APIs and AI.

(Designed to improve portability and reduce unnecessary data lock-in.)

Important distinction

The open analytical layer is not intended to replace the transactional database responsible for safely processing live orders, inventory changes, approvals or payments.
